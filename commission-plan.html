Plan 02 • Performance-Based Collaboration

Commission Plan

A performance-based engagement model where compensation is connected to agreed commercial outcomes rather than relying entirely on a traditional upfront project fee.

GUNKOWII SABA • E-COMMERCE & DIGITAL

What Is the Commission Plan?

Instead of paying only for a fixed project, a business and I can agree on a commission structure where my compensation is connected to defined results, such as qualifying sales or orders generated during the agreed engagement.

The Important Part

This is not a promise of guaranteed sales. Performance depends on many factors, including the product, pricing, market demand, store quality, traffic, advertising budget, fulfillment, customer trust, and the agreed strategy.

How It Works

A successful commission arrangement needs clear expectations before the work begins.

01

Evaluate

We review the store, products, current position, traffic, marketing situation, and business objectives.

02

Agree

We agree on the scope, qualifying results, commission structure, tracking method, timeframe, and responsibilities.

03

Execute

I work on the agreed areas such as store optimization, CRO, SEO, marketing, or customer acquisition.

04

Measure

Performance is reviewed using the agreed tracking method and compensation is handled according to the agreed terms.

Benefits of the Commission Plan

Lower Upfront Commitment

Depending on the agreed arrangement, the business may not need to make the same type of upfront investment required by a traditional expert engagement.

Shared Performance Focus

The model creates a strong focus on commercial outcomes because compensation is connected to the agreed performance criteria.

Good for Testing Collaboration

It can provide an alternative for businesses that want to explore a working relationship before committing to a larger fixed project.

Growth-Oriented Approach

The strategy can focus on the complete path from traffic and customer experience through conversion and retention.

Advantages & Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Potentially lower upfront cost
  • Strong focus on agreed commercial outcomes
  • Can align incentives between both sides
  • Useful for businesses comfortable with performance-based arrangements
  • Can combine strategy, optimization, and marketing

Disadvantages & Risks

  • Income depends on the agreed performance criteria
  • Results cannot be guaranteed
  • Sales may depend on factors outside my control
  • Attribution can become complicated without proper tracking
  • Both sides need clear responsibilities and reporting
Important: A commission arrangement should never be based on vague promises. Before starting, both sides should clearly define what counts as a qualifying result, how results will be tracked, what timeframe applies, and what happens when external factors affect performance.

Who Should Consider This Plan?

This model is not ideal for every business. It works best when the business and project are suitable for measurable performance tracking.

Businesses With a Sellable Product

The product or service should have a clear market, reasonable pricing, and a genuine opportunity to attract customers.

Businesses Ready to Track Results

The business should be willing to provide access to the relevant information required to measure agreed outcomes accurately.

Businesses Open to Collaboration

Store owners still have responsibilities involving pricing, inventory, fulfillment, customer service, and other operational areas.

Businesses Seeking Growth

This can suit businesses that want to focus heavily on customer acquisition, conversion, and measurable commercial improvement.

What Must Be Clear Before Starting?

Area What Should Be Agreed
Commission The percentage or compensation structure.
Qualifying result Exactly which sales, orders, or outcomes count.
Tracking How qualifying results will be identified and verified.
Timeframe When the performance arrangement begins and ends.
Responsibilities What I handle and what the business owner remains responsible for.
External factors How refunds, cancellations, inventory shortages, or other factors are treated.

Commission Does Not Mean “No Investment”

A performance-based arrangement may reduce or change the upfront professional fee, but the business may still need to invest in areas such as advertising, inventory, software, fulfillment, content, payment processing, or other operating requirements.

The purpose is to change how professional compensation is structured, not to suggest that e-commerce growth requires no investment.
