Trial Plan: Test Before You Commit

A practical way to evaluate the working relationship

The Trial Plan gives a business the opportunity to start with a clearly defined, limited-scope engagement before moving into a larger long-term project or another working arrangement.

01 • UNDERSTAND THE MODEL

What Is the Trial Plan?

The Trial Plan is a limited engagement designed to allow both sides to evaluate the quality of the work, communication, process and working relationship before committing to a broader arrangement.

Start small. Evaluate properly.

Not every business wants to commit immediately to a large project. A trial can provide a practical starting point where a specific problem, task or opportunity is selected for focused attention.

The trial should have a clearly defined scope, timeframe, deliverables and expectations. Once the trial is completed, both sides can review the work and decide whether continuing makes sense.

This makes the Trial Plan particularly useful when the main question is not simply "Can this work?" but also "Are we a good fit to work together?"

What the trial is not

A trial is not a guarantee of sales, rankings, advertising performance or immediate revenue growth.

Its primary purpose is to test a defined piece of work and evaluate the professional working relationship under real project conditions.

02 • THE TRIAL PROCESS

How the Trial Plan Works

The goal is to keep the first engagement focused enough to evaluate properly without creating unnecessary complexity.

01

Identify the Objective

We identify one specific area that needs attention and determine what the trial should accomplish.

02

Define the Scope

We agree on what is included, what is not included, the expected deliverables and the trial timeframe.

03

Begin the Work

The agreed task or improvement is carried out using the same practical approach applied to larger projects.

04

Review the Work

We review the completed work, observations, improvements, challenges and any available performance indicators.

05

Evaluate the Relationship

Both sides consider communication, responsiveness, quality, process and whether expectations were properly met.

06

Decide What Comes Next

Continue, expand, change the engagement model or conclude the trial. There is no assumption that continuation is automatic.

04 • BENEFITS

Why Consider a Trial?

A well-designed trial reduces uncertainty by allowing both sides to work together before deciding on a larger engagement.

01

Lower Commitment

A limited engagement can be easier to consider than immediately committing to a large project.

02

Test the Working Style

The business gets to experience communication, process, responsiveness and delivery firsthand.

03

Evaluate Quality

The business can evaluate the quality of actual work rather than relying only on promises or portfolio descriptions.

04

Focused Objective

A smaller scope makes it easier to concentrate on one meaningful problem or opportunity.

05

Better Decision Making

The results of the trial can provide additional information before deciding whether to expand the relationship.

06

Flexible Next Step

After the trial, the business can choose the arrangement that best fits its objectives and budget.

05 • MAKE AN INFORMED DECISION

Advantages & Limitations

A trial is useful because it reduces uncertainty, but it also has limits that should be understood before starting.

Advantages

  • Lower level of commitment compared with a large project.
  • Allows the client to evaluate actual work.
  • Creates an opportunity to test communication and collaboration.
  • Provides a practical basis for deciding whether to continue.
  • Makes it easier to start with one clearly defined priority.

Disadvantages & Limitations

  • A short trial may not be long enough to demonstrate every potential business outcome.
  • Some SEO and marketing activities require more time before their full impact can reasonably be evaluated.
  • A limited scope cannot address every issue in a store.
  • Results may be influenced by factors outside the trial scope.
  • A successful trial does not automatically mean every larger project will produce the same outcome.
06 • IS IT RIGHT FOR YOU?

Who Should Consider the Trial Plan?

The Trial Plan is particularly useful for businesses that want to experience the working relationship before making a larger commitment.

Good Fit

  • Businesses that have never worked with me before.
  • Owners who want to evaluate the quality of the work first.
  • Stores with one clear problem or opportunity to address.
  • Businesses that prefer a smaller first engagement.
  • Owners who want to test communication and collaboration.
  • Businesses considering a larger future project.

May Not Be the Best Fit

  • Businesses expecting a complete store transformation from a limited trial.
  • Projects where meaningful evaluation requires a much longer timeframe.
  • Businesses expecting guaranteed sales from a small engagement.
  • Owners who are not prepared to define a clear objective.
  • Businesses that need a full long-term strategy immediately.
  • Projects where the required work cannot reasonably be divided into a limited scope.
07 • SET THE RIGHT EXPECTATIONS

What Should You Expect From a Trial?

The strongest trial is specific, measurable where possible and realistic about what can be achieved within the agreed scope.

A Defined Scope

You should know exactly what work is being performed before the trial begins.

Clear Deliverables

The expected output should be understood by both sides rather than being left open-ended.

Realistic Evaluation

Some improvements can be evaluated immediately while others require additional time or performance data.

Honest Feedback

Both sides should be able to discuss what worked, what did not and what should change.

No Forced Continuation

The trial is an evaluation stage. Continuing into a larger project should be a mutual decision.

A Clear Next Step

If both sides want to continue, the next engagement can be structured around the lessons learned from the trial.

What Happens After the Trial?

Once the trial is complete, we review the work and decide what makes the most sense for the business. There is no need to force the next stage into the same structure.

Continue Continue with a broader project if the relationship and results justify it.
Change Plan Move into the Expert, Commission or Reward structure where appropriate.
Conclude Finish the trial if the business does not require additional work.
